About

What I do best is take a broken, manual process, find the structural cause, design the fix and build the automated process, with quality gates before anything goes live. Automation, to me, is about freeing people from the repetitive stuff so their time can go to what actually creates value. I have done that in regulated financial services my whole career.

A recent example from Forca, where I serve as Release Train Engineer: at every month-end, capacity had to be reconciled against realised hours by hand. The reconciliation was complex, and the result was contested. I built a Python application for the analysis, using GitHub Copilot agents in defined SDLC roles. The month-end now gives one consistent, objective report instead of conflicting assessments.

I have led IT development in regulated financial services for most of my career, with and without formal line management, and have built development teams myself, local and distributed. The best deliveries I have been part of came from teams built on trust, engagement and shared ownership: colleagues who make each other better. That is the collaboration I look for, and the one I take responsibility for creating.

The AI projects

Over the past year I have built three AI systems as personal projects. I built them to understand the possibilities and the limitations, and because it fascinates me so much that I cannot leave it alone.

I'm not a software engineer. In these projects I am the architect, the designer and the quality assurance, and AI writes the code. They are not demos. They are working systems with evaluation frameworks, audit trails, and quality gates.

The point was never the code itself. It was to develop genuine, first-hand understanding of what it takes to deliver AI responsibly, and to be able to ask the right questions when it matters.
